## A repeating sequence of simple rules creates a universal computer

The famous [Rule 110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_110) of [Wolfram's elementary cellular automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_cellular_automaton) has a lot of interesting properties. It shows gliders, 'chaos' and is Turing complete, which means it is capable of universal computation.

However, rule 110 is slightly difficult to explain, as it's not symmetric and differentiates left from right. Symmetric rules, on the other hand, fail to give any complex behavior.

But what if you can use a diverse selection of simple rules, in order to get the complex behavior?

That's studied here, and it turns out rules 36, 37, and 5 together can perfectly emulate 110!
